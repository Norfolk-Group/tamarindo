# 03 — The Ten-Year Plan: Growth and Use of Capital

*Phases are OPINION built on sourced anchors: $10M + $10M InterVest
commitment (Aug 20), ~40–50 homes at $700–800k average with 60% max LTV
(Aug 19), lean team of ~5, no capital exclusivity, autos deferred.*

Two kinds of capital, never confused:

- **Vehicle capital** buys properties. It belongs to funding partners,
  earns the base yield, scales with demand. Target: ~$20M → ~$1B.
- **OpCo capital** funds Tamarindo US to breakeven. It belongs to equity
  investors. Target: one seed round (~$2.5–3.5M, see 05), ideally the
  only equity ever needed.

## Phase 0 — Prove the paper (now → ~Q1 2027)

The work already in flight: legal opinions (lease characterization, usury,
sucursal tax), contract stack drafted and reviewed, financial model built
(Ricardo's assignment from the debrief), investor deck, servicing platform
v1, and the first 3 ICPs defined (see 04).

**Gate to Phase 1:** clean-enough legal opinions + first vehicle docs
signed + 3 properties under diligence.

## Phase 1 — The $20M pilot (Years 1–2)

Deploy InterVest's capital: ~$10M Medellín, ~$10M Cartagena, ~40–50 homes
at ~$450k average funded. Tight box per Mike Gontar: prime borrowers only,
ICP properties only, no exceptions for volume. Stand up Ashoka's rental
operation on the first pooled units — the rental-offset story must be
*demonstrated*, not promised, by the end of this phase.

**What the pilot must answer (the kill/scale criteria):**

1. Conversion: do qualified prospects sign at a 10–12% client rate?
2. Payment behavior: delinquency inside underwriting assumptions?
3. Recovery: at least one full recovery path exercised or credibly
   fire-drilled end to end.
4. Rental economics: does the pool actually offset 30–55% of payments?
5. Unit ops cost: can 5 people + platform service 50 homes?

**End state:** ~$20M AUM, ~45 homes, fee income running ~$0.8–1.2M/yr
(see 05), a data room that proves the machine works.

## Phase 2 — Multiply the vehicles (Years 2–4)

With pilot proof: InterVest follow-on plus 1–2 new capital partners on
cloned vehicles ("Tamarindo-[Partner] LLC"). Target ~$60–80M cumulative
AUM by end of Year 3 — the OpCo breakeven zone. Expand the box carefully:
second-tier neighborhoods within the same cities, then a third Colombian
market (ASSUMPTION: Bogotá or the coffee axis) only after ICP data
supports it. Revisit the deferred autos decision with real portfolio data.

**Use of capital:** the seed round is consumed here — team to ~10–12,
platform hardening, legal templates for multi-vehicle reporting.

## Phase 3 — The funding marketplace (Years 4–7)

The template vehicle becomes a product: standardized docs, standardized
reporting, competitive capital pricing. 4–6 vehicles, ~$150–400M AUM.
Second corridor opens (ASSUMPTION: Mexico — same diaspora logic, larger
market) with Tamarindo Colombia's playbook franchised into a Tamarindo
Mexico. Ashoka scales to hundreds of managed units and may take external
(non-Tamarindo) properties to densify operations in each city.

**Watch item (OPINION):** at this size, structured-finance options appear
(warehouse lines, forward-flow agreements, eventually rated
securitization of lease streams). Cheaper capital → wider spread → but
also regulatory weight. Take it deliberately, not opportunistically.

## Phase 4 — The default rails (Years 7–10)

Target end state: ~$0.7–1B AUM across 2–3 countries, 8–10 funding
vehicles, 1,500+ properties under Ashoka management. Tamarindo US earns
~3% of AUM annually across the stack (~$20–30M revenue) with servicer-like
margins. The company is the answer to "finance a foreign home with
domestic credit" the way specific companies became the answer to
cross-border payroll or payments.

**Strategic options at this point (not plans):** bank partnership or
charter, expansion into other hard assets on the same rails, or exit to a
specialty-finance acquirer who values the fee stream and the moat.

control surface that keeps acquisition quality constant while volume
grows.
