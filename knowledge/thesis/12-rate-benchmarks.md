# 12 — Rate Benchmarks: What the Market Charges vs Our 11.84%

*Researched 2026-08-23 (web, live quotes and published rate sheets). The
question: what is a realistic lease rate on a ~$600k Colombian asset at
60% LTV ($360k funded) for a prime US borrower? Every figure below cites
its source class; re-verify before investor use.*

## Verdict (OPINION)

Our **11.84% blended property rate now sits just above the top of the
defensible band** — be honest about that. The realistic band for this
borrower and structure is **9.5–11.5%**: Mexico cross-border USD pricing
(8–10%) plus a Colombia premium of ~100–150 bps for thinner resale,
sovereign spread, and no competing USD product. At 11.84% we are ~35 bps
over it, and that is a deliberate trade: at 11% flat the vehicle IRR'd
below Intervest's 8.5% floor (see below). Against the client's real local
alternative — Colombian leasing habitacional at 14%+ EA **in pesos** —
11.84% USD still wins clearly. Against US anchors (6.65% conventional) it
needs the story told right: no other USD product exists for Colombian
property.

## The Ferrari datapoint (FACT)

Exotic car leases are **cheaper than people assume**: money factors
0.0011–0.0035 → **2.6–8.4% APR equivalent**. Lamborghini Financial ~2.8%;
Premier Financial Services / Putnam-style open-end balloon leases to 720+
clients price mid-to-high single digits. Why so low: US-titled collateral,
weekend repossession, 50–60% residuals, deep auction market.

**Implication (OPINION):** our 14.5% USD auto rate is justified by
Colombian recovery risk and the local bank alternative (17–28% EA), *not*
by the asset. A US client who has leased a Porsche at ~7% will feel the
gap. Sales copy must anchor autos against Colombian banks, never US car
leases. Aircraft at 9.5% is consistent with prime aviation finance.

Intervest's stated appetite band is 8.5–11.5% (thesis 09). Therefore:

- **11% + 20% strip** → bottom of band or under it. This *was* the base and

- **Current base is 11.5% + FICO blend = 11.84% + 20% strip** → vehicle
  **9.08%**, comfortably inside the band. See the grid below.
- **Discounting below base costs Tamarindo, not Intervest.** The "excellent
  credit" discount is now delivered as the **810+ tier at −25 bps**, not as
  a strip cut — the strip stays at 20%.
- **9.5% does not work** for the vehicle at any strip we would accept.

## Credit score and rate (policy — Ricardo 2026-08-23)

**Rates tie to the lessee's US FICO.** Intervest sets rate policy and
asked us to "be reasonable"; Ricardo's posture: **close to hard money
(10–16%) but not quite there**. FICO 750+ / Tier 1 / SSN remains the
admission gate; above the gate, three pricing tiers ride on each ICP's
base rate:

| FICO tier | Spread vs ICP base | Share of book (ASSUMPTION) |
|---|---|---|
| 750–779 | +75 bps | 50% |
| 780–809 | base | 35% |
| 810+ | −25 bps | 15% |

Blended spread ≈ **+33.75 bps**. With base rates stepped up 50 bps
(ICP-1 at 11.5%), the blended property rate lands ≈ **11.84%** — inside
the lower hard-money band but packaged as a lease with a 20%-of-asset
purchase option, not a loan. All tier shares and spreads are model
variables users can what-if.

**Why this exists: the vehicle was too thin.** At 11.0% flat with the
20% interest strip, Intervest's IRR was **8.35%** — below their 8.5–11.5%
band. At 11.5% base + FICO blend, keeping the 20% strip and 75 bps
servicing, the vehicle IRRs ≈ **9.1%** and Tamarindo's spread+servicing
take per ICP-1 deal rises to ~$57.6k. ICP-1 payment moves $3,084 →
**$3,223/mo** — still below the pre-balloon-raise $3,278.

The thesis-06 open question stands: will a 750+ borrower pay ~11–12% in
dollars? Demand question; the first cohort answers it.
