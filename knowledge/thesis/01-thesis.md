# 01 — The Thesis: What Tamarindo Should Be

*Synthesized from the founding thesis (Aug 7), the Aug 18 investor debrief,
the Aug 19 team call, and Granola meetings Jun 8 – Aug 20. Opinions are
labeled OPINION; unverified numbers are labeled ASSUMPTION.*

## The problem (established across all sources)

A prime US borrower — 750+ FICO, strong income, clean history — walks into
a Colombian bank to buy a $700k apartment in Cartagena and is treated as a
stranger. Local financing is unavailable or punitive; US banks won't lend
against foreign collateral. The result is an all-cash market for exactly
the buyers who least need to pay cash. Credit does not travel, even when
the borrower's quality does.

## What Tamarindo is

**A credit translation layer.** Tamarindo converts US creditworthiness
into purchasing power for Colombian hard assets through a lease-to-own
structure: a US funding vehicle buys the property through its Colombian
sucursal, the client gets immediate use rights (comodato) plus a purchase
option, and pays a US-law lease over ~10 years with a residual balloon.

**OPINION — the three identities, in order of importance:**

1. **A fee machine, not a lender.** Tamarindo never takes balance-sheet
   risk on properties. Capital partners (InterVest first) own the assets
   and earn the base yield; Tamarindo earns layered fees on every stage of
   the asset's life: origination, activation, servicing, interest spread,
   property management, and rental share. The company should be valued on
   fee income per dollar of assets under management, like a servicer or a
   marketplace — not like a REIT.

2. **A rails company.** The durable asset is the *legal + operational
   machinery*: the sucursal structure, the comodato + US lease + purchase
   option contract stack, the recovery playbook, the underwriting policy,
   and the servicing platform. Once proven in Colombia, the same rails
   carry other capital partners, other asset classes (autos were
   deliberately deferred, not rejected), and other countries.

3. **A lifestyle product.** The client story is not "get a loan" — it is
   "use your US credit to own your place in Colombia, and let it earn rent
   when you're not there." The rental pool (Aug 19 decision: Tamarindo
   operates rentals, keeps ~20%) turns the property from a liability into
   a partially self-funding asset. This is the emotional core of the pitch
   and the practical core of client affordability.

## Why this wins (OPINION)

- **The moat is legal-operational, not technological.** Anyone can copy a
  landing page; almost no one will assemble Colombian sucursal ownership,
  bilingual contract stacks, notary/title workflows, and a tested recovery
  path. Every closed deal deepens the moat.
- **The capital model is replicable by design.** Tamarindo-Intervest is
  vehicle #1 of N. Because Tamarindo kept no exclusivity with InterVest
  (Aug 19), each new capital partner gets a clone vehicle on the same
  rails. At scale this is a *funding marketplace*: capital competes to
  fund Tamarindo-originated assets, compressing Tamarindo's cost of
  capital and raising its spread.
- **The service layer compounds.** Sister company Ashoka captures
  maintenance, property management, and rental operations at market rates.
  The family of companies earns on the asset's entire life, not just its
  financing.
- **Demand is validated where it counts.** The debrief's honest position:
  the pain point is real and widely evidenced; willingness-to-pay at
  10–12% is the open question. The pilot is sized precisely to answer it.

## What Tamarindo is NOT (guardrails from the sources)

- Not a mortgage lender, not a bank, not a securities issuer to clients —
  legal characterization of the lease matters and has open items
  (usury/consumer-credit review is on the pre-launch checklist).
- Not a property developer or speculator. Appreciation accrues mostly to
  the client via the purchase option; Tamarindo's returns must not depend
  on price appreciation.
- Not asset-heavy. If Tamarindo ever owns properties on its own balance
  sheet beyond incidental/recovery timing windows, the model has drifted.
- Not a volume-at-any-cost originator. Mike Gontar's advice (Aug 20)
  stands: limit the test, keep the box tight, prove the mechanics.

## The bet, stated plainly (OPINION)

Prime expat/diaspora demand for Colombian lifestyle assets is deep enough
to absorb ~45 homes in 18 months at a ~10–12% client rate, and the rental
pool offsets enough of the payment that the effective cost feels closer to
6–7%. If that clears, Tamarindo's rails plus a multiplying set of funding
vehicles reach ~$60M AUM by Year 3 (OpCo breakeven), several hundred
million by Year 7, and the company becomes the default answer to "how do I
finance a home abroad with my US credit?" — earning roughly 3% of AUM
annually across the fee and service stack.

The kill criteria are equally plain: if pilot conversion at viable pricing
fails, or the recovery path proves unenforceable in practice, the thesis
does not scale and the test was sized so that failure is affordable.
