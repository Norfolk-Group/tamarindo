# 09 — Operating and Capital Assumptions

*Use these defaults in the 10-year cash-flow model. Treat FACT as a cited
observation, OPINION as a recommended policy, and ASSUMPTION as a model input
that must be replaced by a quote, contract, or payroll calculation. USD unless
stated otherwise. Research current through 2026-08-23.*

## 1. Vehicle capital after the $20M test

- **FACT — internal deal record:** vehicle #1 provides $10M plus a conditional
  $10M follow-on at a **9–12% vehicle yield**. Tamarindo earns **2% activation**
  on drawdown and **about 20% of interest billings**. Keep any separate
  servicing fee distinct. Public InterVest pages do not confirm these Tamarindo
  terms; retain the meeting/source file behind the thesis.
- **FACT — public fit:** InterVest describes direct structured-finance
  investments tied to leased or rented physical assets and private-credit
  investments in specialty-finance businesses. It reports 23 platform
  investments and $21.3B+ annual originations
  ([InterVest Specialty Finance](https://intervest.com/specialty-finance/),
  accessed 2026-08-23).
- **FACT — distinguish the economics:** “2 and 20” means an investment manager
  may charge its LPs roughly 2% annual management fee plus 20% carried interest
  on profits. It is not the asset yield and it is not Tamarindo's activation or
  spread share ([SEC glossary](https://www.sec.gov/resources-small-businesses/glossary),
  accessed 2026-08-23).
- **ASSUMPTION — model the capital partner:** pay 9–12% on average funded
  vehicle capital. Do **not** add 2+20 again unless signed vehicle documents
  charge those fees on top of the stated yield.
- **ASSUMPTION — model Tamarindo US:** collect 2% once when capital is drawn;
  collect 20% of the interest component billed to the customer; collect a
  separate **75 bps annual servicing fee on outstanding principal** (research
  seed; market check below is 75–200 bps). WhatsApp / slide 40 bps is a
  complementary ask, not the live default
  ([19-platform-economics.md](19-platform-economics.md)).
- **FACT — market check:** 2025–26 equipment-finance benchmarks put starter
  warehouses at SOFR +225–450 bps, scaling lines at SOFR +150–350 bps, and
  forward-flow servicing at 75–200 bps. Forward-flow asset yields commonly
  range 8.5–13.5% by credit tier
  ([Monitor Daily](https://www.monitordaily.com/article/funding-an-independent-leasing-company-today-a-20-year-evolution/),
  accessed 2026-08-23). This supports, but does not prove, the 9–12% input.

## 2. Capital partners 2, 3, and 4

- **FACT:** forward-flow funders commonly demand minimum volumes, allocation
  rights, and sometimes ROFRs on new products or facilities. Originators must
  preserve room for multiple funders
  ([Weil, 2025-10-21](https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/fund-management-reits/1694376/forward-flow-transactions-a-key-tool-in-fuelling-private-credit-growth),
  accessed 2026-08-23).
- **OPINION:** grant a narrow ROFR, not blanket exclusivity. Limit it to an
  agreed credit box, corridor, and short match window. Release unallocated
  production automatically. Make exclusivity step down when the partner misses
  deployment, approval-time, or renewal targets.
- **ASSUMPTION — years 4–5 / partner 2:** use **8.5–11.5%** vehicle yield.
- **ASSUMPTION — years 6–7 / partner 3:** use **8.0–11.0%** vehicle yield.
- **ASSUMPTION — years 8–10 / partner 4:** use **7.5–10.5%** vehicle yield.
- **ASSUMPTION:** tighten only 25–75 bps per proven cohort and never below live
  benchmark funding plus required credit enhancement. Keep activation,
  servicing, and spread economics flat in the base case; show any fee give-up
  in a downside case.

## 3. US loaded team cost

- **FACT:** in 2026 the employer pays 6.2% Social Security up to $184,500 and
  1.45% Medicare without a cap; the employer does not match Additional Medicare
  Tax ([IRS Publication 15](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p15), accessed
  2026-08-23).
- **FACT:** 2025 average annual health premiums were $9,325 single and $26,993
  family; workers paid $1,440 and $6,850 respectively. Therefore the implied
  average employer shares were **$7,885 single** and **$20,143 family**
  ([KFF 2025 survey](https://www.kff.org/health-costs/2025-employer-health-benefits-survey/),
  accessed 2026-08-23).
- **ASSUMPTION — use single coverage in the base case:** MD at $25,000/month
  costs **$26,973/month loaded**; Finance at $15,000 costs **$16,805**; Corp Ops
  at $25,000 costs **$26,973**; CTO at $15,000 costs **$16,805**. Total:
  **$87,555/month** or **$1,050,658/year**. These figures include salary,
  employer FICA/Medicare, and $7,885 annual health only.
- **ASSUMPTION — family-health sensitivity:** add **$1,022/month per covered
  role**, taking all four roles to about **$91,641/month**.
- **ASSUMPTION:** add 3–5% of payroll for state unemployment, workers'
  compensation, payroll administration, and jurisdiction variance after the
  employing state is known. Do not count PTO as extra cash unless backfill is
  hired.
- **FACT:** Q4 2025 US flex-workspace medians were about $220/month per open or
  dedicated desk; Manhattan was about $339
  ([Allwork.Space, 2026-02](https://allwork.space/2026/02/coworking-grows-bigger-and-more-selective-across-u-s-markets/),
  accessed 2026-08-23).
- **ASSUMPTION:** budget WFH at **$200/person/month** for stipend, equipment
  amortization, and periodic meetings; budget flex office at
  **$350/person/month** including the desk and shared meeting-room overage.
  The **$2.5M OpCo book** (thesis 21) puts all four US named seats at 100%
  WFH — no US flex desk. Colombia keeps two small offices.
- **ASSUMPTION — remote business expenses:** Dov, Rosario, and Ricardo at
  **$2,000/person/month** (Ricardo's stated run-rate; not $2,000k). Tom at
  **$500/month**. Separate from the $200 WFH stipend and from Colombia travel.
- **ASSUMPTION — US benefits on top of §3 loaded:** 401k match **3% of cash
  salary** and dental/vision **$150/person/month**. Medical and FICA stay
  inside the loaded figures. Every US cash seat gets benefits. Family
  medical remains the +$1,022/month sensitivity, not the base.

## 4. Colombia GM

- **FACT:** 2026 minimum integral salary is COP 22,761,765/month. Salario
  integral must be written, includes a 30% prestaciones factor, excludes
  vacation, and uses 70% of salary as the health/pension/ARL contribution base
  ([UGPP 2026 guide](https://www.ugpp.gov.co/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/ABC-Trabajadores-dependientes-que-devengan-salario-integral.pdf),
  accessed 2026-08-23).
- **FACT:** the 2026 minimum wage is COP 1,750,905 and the social-security base
  is capped at 25 minimum wages
  ([Baker McKenzie, 2026-01](https://www.bakermckenzie.com/en/insight/publications/2026/01/colombia-adjusts-minimum-wage-and-transport-allowance),
  accessed 2026-08-23).
- **ASSUMPTION:** at **$15,000/month salario integral**, budget about
  **$18,150/month loaded**: salary plus roughly 21% for employer health,
  pension, low-risk ARL, and parafiscales applied to the 70% base. Recalculate
  in COP each year and obtain Colombian payroll advice.
- **ASSUMPTION:** an ordinary contract at the same $15,000 gross salary costs
  roughly **$22,500–$22,800/month** after prima, cesantías, cesantías interest,
  vacation, social security, ARL, and parafiscales. For equal headline total
  compensation, compare integral $15,000 with ordinary base
  **$11,538 ($15,000 / 1.30)**, loaded at roughly **$17,500/month**.

## 7. Ashoka short-term rental management

- **FACT:** 2026 market guides place full-service STR management in Medellín
  and Cartagena around **15–25% of gross rent**, normally excluding some
  cleaning, supplies, and major repairs
  ([Medellín](https://thelatinvestor.com/blogs/news/medellin-airbnb);
  [Cartagena](https://thelatinvestor.com/blogs/news/cartagena-airbnb),
  accessed 2026-08-23).
- **ASSUMPTION:** charge Ashoka **18–22% of gross STR rent**; use **20%** in
  the base case. Model cleaning, platform fees, utilities, HOA, taxes,
  maintenance, and Tamarindo's separate rental share outside that fee.
- **OPINION:** benchmark and disclose Ashoka's related-party fee annually.
  Make the contract terminable for non-performance and do not bury operating
  costs inside the management percentage.
